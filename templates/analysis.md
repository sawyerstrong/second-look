# Analysis: <title>

Replace each italic prompt with your own text. Delete a section only if it does not apply, and say why in one line. If the assessment gives its own format, use that and fit these sections into it.

## 1. Plan (write before looking at results)

- *Question:* the one question this analysis answers.
- *Main outcome:* the measurement that answers it.
- *Unit of replication:* what counts as one independent observation (a separate sample, run, or subject; not a repeat measurement of one).
- *Planned method:* the test or model, and why.
- *Exclusion criteria:* decided now, before seeing which points they would remove.

If the plan changes after you see the data, keep the original here and note what changed and why.

## 2. Experimental methods

*How the data was generated, in enough detail that someone could repeat it or change one thing:*

- *Procedure:* the protocol as actually run, with any deviations from the written version.
- *Conditions and controls:* what was varied, what was held fixed, and the positive and negative controls.
- *Materials:* reagents or inputs with lot numbers, preparation dates, and storage.
- *Instruments:* which ones, settings, and calibration status.
- *Layout and order:* plate or batch layout, run order, dates, and who ran each part.

These details are where a problem usually gets fixed, and where confounds hide. Record them even if they seem routine.

## 3. Data

*Source, file names, row and column counts, units, and how the samples were collected or chosen. Anything missing or odd in the raw files.*

## 4. Assumptions

*Each assumption the analysis depends on, whether you checked it, and how.*

## 5. Analysis methods

*What you ran, the alternatives you considered, and why you chose this one. Name the script that reproduces everything.*

## 6. Exclusions

*Each removed point or sample: count, reason, and the main result with and without it.*

## 7. Results

*Actual computed values with uncertainty (and what the uncertainty is: SD, SE, CI). Figures with labeled axes and units. Mark each claim measured or inferred.*

## 8. Limitations

*What this data cannot show. Sample size, design gaps, and the counter-arguments you could not rule out.*

## 9. Conclusion

*One or two sentences, worded no more strongly than the results earn.*

## 10. Next experiment

*Hypothesis, the one experimental-method variable from section 2 you would change, what stays fixed, the controls, and which result would rule each explanation in or out.*
